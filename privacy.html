Privacy policy

Website Page Auditor · Last updated: November 20, 2026

Website Page Auditor has one purpose: audit the webpage you choose and help you find and review quality issues. This policy explains what the extension reads, how it uses and stores that information, and which requests optional checks make. The extension follows the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements: website content is used only to provide the auditing features described here, and is not sold or transferred for unrelated purposes.

Summary

  • The extension reads a page only after you accept its in-product notice and use a page feature on that tab.
  • Page content and audit findings are processed on your device. They are not sent to the developer, an extension server, or an analytics service.
  • There are no accounts, analytics, advertising, tracking, or remotely hosted executable code.
  • Website content is not sold or shared. If you choose the exit survey, only the feedback you enter is passed to Gmail so you can prepare an email.

Information the extension reads

After you agree to the notice, the extension reads content from the tab where you use a page feature. This is website content under the Chrome Web Store data categories and may include:

  • the page URL and title;
  • visible text, HTML structure, computed styles, and element sizes and positions;
  • links and image URLs, and page metadata such as og:image;
  • form labels, field types, and validation settings.

The extension does not read text you type into form fields, passwords, cookies, browsing history across tabs, or pages you did not choose to audit. The optional form check may enter its own sample values and restore the original fields afterward; it does not save either set of values.

How information is used and stored

Page information is used to generate the audit, show evidence and suggested fixes, and highlight selected elements. It is processed locally in Chrome and is not transmitted to the developer or another server.

Optional exit survey

If you choose “Why are you leaving?” before uninstalling, the reasons you select and any comment you write are placed in a Gmail compose draft addressed to example@gmail.com (a temporary address that the publisher must replace before release). This is an HTTPS link to Gmail; Google receives the information to prepare the draft. The extension does not send the email. You can review, edit, or discard the draft, and the publisher receives feedback only if you press Send. Do not include sensitive information. Gmail's privacy policy and retention practices apply to the draft.

Requests made by optional checks

Optional interactive and status-check features are off by default. You can enable them in Settings. The extension has no server of its own. Image checks may reload images already referenced by the audited page; other requests go to that page or to link destinations you choose to check.

Your choices

  • Choose “Not now” in the notice and the extension will not read a page.
  • Keep optional interactive and network checks off, or enable only the checks you want in Settings.
  • Use “Delete this report” to remove a tab's findings.
  • Use Settings → Privacy → “Withdraw consent and delete stored audit data” to remove stored reports and require consent again before the next page feature.
  • Remove the extension to delete its settings.

Sharing and Limited Use

Website content, findings, settings, and images referenced by the audited page are not shared with the developer or third parties. The optional exit survey is user-initiated and sends only the selected reasons and comment to Gmail to prepare a draft; the publisher receives it only if you send the email. Feedback is used only to improve the extension. The extension does not sell user data or use it for advertising, profiling, credit decisions, or purposes unrelated to its single purpose. Its use of website content complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Limits of the audit

Automated checks find common problems on one page as it appears at that moment. They do not test a whole site, guarantee accessibility or compliance with any standard, or replace human review.
